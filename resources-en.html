📊 Valuation Metrics

📊
PE Ratio（市盈率）
Share price divided by earnings per share. In theory, a lower PE means cheaper, but always compare it with the industry average.
Valuation
📐
PB Ratio（市净率）
Share price divided by net assets per share. A PB below 1 means the stock trades below its book value. Especially useful for bank stocks.
Valuation
💹
ROE（股本回报率）
How much profit a company makes from shareholders’ money. A higher ROE means capital is used more efficiently. Above 15% is generally considered strong.
Profitability
📈
EPS（每股盈利）
Net profit divided by total shares. Growing EPS is a key sign of improving profitability.
Earnings
💰
Dividend Yield（股息率）
Dividend per share divided by share price, shown as a percentage. Above 4% is considered high in Malaysia.
Dividends
🏦
Market Cap（市值）
Share price × total shares = the company’s total market value. Malaysian stocks are grouped into large caps (above RM1 billion), mid caps and small caps.
Size

🏢 Investment Products

🏢
REIT（房地产信托）
Invest in property through a listed security. Most income is paid out to unitholders as distributions, which suits investors seeking steady income.

ETF（交易所交易基金）
A basket of stocks packaged into one product traded on the exchange. Low cost and diversified, which makes it beginner friendly.

Unit Trust（单位信托）
An investment fund run by a fund manager. Fees are higher than ETFs, but you get professional management.

Shariah-Compliant Stock
Stocks that comply with Islamic principles, with no involvement in alcohol, tobacco, gambling and similar businesses. Bursa publishes a dedicated Shariah-compliant list.

💳
Warrant（认股权证）
The right to buy the underlying shares at a fixed price within a set period. A derivative with higher risk, not suitable for beginners.

🏗️ Corporate Actions

🚀
IPO（首次公开募股）
A company lists on Bursa for the first time and the public can apply for new shares. The price can rise sharply or fall after listing.

⚖️
Rights Issue（附加股）
A company raises money by issuing new shares to existing shareholders. You can buy at a discounted price, or give up or sell your rights.
Corporate exercises → Read the guide ↗
🎁
Bonus Issue（红股）
A company gives existing shareholders extra shares for free. You hold more shares but the total value stays the same, so the price usually adjusts down.
Corporate exercises
🤝
Private Placement（私人配售）
A company issues new shares to selected investors without a public offer. Usually dilutive, so watch the announcements.
Corporate exercises
✂️
Stock Split（股票分割）
One share is split into several to lower the price so more people can afford it. You hold more shares at a proportionally lower price.
Corporate exercises
💸
Ex-Dividend Date（除息日）
If you buy on or after the ex-date, you will not receive that dividend. The share price usually drops by a similar amount on the ex-date.
Dividends

📉 Market Concepts

🐂
Bull Market（牛市）
A period when the market rises more than 20%. Investor confidence is high and the economy is usually doing well.
Market trends
🐻
Bear Market（熊市）
A period when the market falls more than 20% from its peak. For long-term investors, it can also be a chance to buy good companies.
Market trends
🚀
Limit Up（涨停）
Bursa’s maximum daily price rise. Once a stock hits it, the price cannot go higher that day, a sign of extreme buying.
Trading rules
📉
Limit Down（跌停）
The maximum daily price fall. Hitting it signals heavy selling pressure, so look carefully at the reason.
Trading rules
📋
Short Selling（沽空）
Borrowing shares to sell, then buying them back after the price falls to return them. Very high risk, not for beginners.
Trading strategy
🔄
Dollar Cost Averaging
Investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, whether the market is up or down. Over time it can lower your average cost, which suits beginners.
Investing strategy

GLOSSARY

Heard a term and had no idea what it means?

You are not alone. These are the most common investing terms you will hear when following Malaysian stocks. Click any term to see a plain-language explanation.

The total value of a company as the stock market sees it. Calculated by multiplying the share price by the total number of shares. Example: if a company has 1 billion shares at RM2 each, its market cap is RM2 billion. Bigger market cap generally means a more established company.
PE (Price-to-Earnings) tells you how many years it would take for the company to earn back what you paid for the share, based on current profits. A PE of 10x means you are paying RM10 for every RM1 of earnings.

Forward PE uses the estimated future earnings instead of past earnings. It is more useful because markets are forward-looking. If a company is expected to grow profits significantly, the forward PE will be lower than the trailing PE, which makes it look cheaper.
Earnings Per Share. The company's total profit divided by the number of shares. If a company earns RM100 million and has 500 million shares, the EPS is 20 sen. Higher EPS generally means the company is more profitable per share. Watch out for diluted EPS, which accounts for warrants and convertible bonds that could increase the share count.
How much cash a company pays you every year as a percentage of the share price. If a stock costs RM2 and pays 10 sen dividend per year, the dividend yield is 5%. A higher yield is attractive, but always check if the company can sustain it. A very high yield sometimes signals the share price has dropped for a reason.
Profit After Tax. The company's bottom-line profit after all expenses, interest, and taxes have been deducted. This is the number most people refer to when they say a company 'made RM50 million in profit.' It is the clearest measure of how much the company actually earned.
YoY (Year-on-Year): comparing this quarter or year to the same period last year. Useful because it removes seasonal effects. If Q2 2025 profit is compared to Q2 2024, that is YoY.

QoQ (Quarter-on-Quarter): comparing this quarter to the previous quarter. Useful for spotting short-term momentum, but can be misleading for seasonal businesses.
Unusual Market Activity. When a stock's price or volume moves sharply without an obvious reason, the stock exchange issues a UMA query, asking the company to explain. The company must respond publicly. A UMA is sometimes a warning sign of speculation or insider activity. Always check what the company says in its UMA response before reacting.
The maximum a stock is allowed to move in a single trading day. On Bursa Malaysia, a stock can move up or down by a maximum of 30% in one day. If it hits the upper limit, it is limit up (涨停). If it hits the lower limit, it is limit down (跌停). Trading may continue at that price but cannot go beyond it.
The cut-off date to be eligible for a dividend or corporate exercise. If you buy shares on or after the ex-date, you will NOT receive the upcoming dividend. You must own the shares before the ex-date. On the ex-date itself, the share price typically drops by roughly the dividend amount because new buyers are no longer entitled to it.
Not the same as a regular bank. An investment bank advises companies on big financial decisions, like getting listed on the stock exchange, buying or selling other companies, or raising money from investors. They do not take deposits from the public. In Malaysia, examples include Maybank IB, CIMB, RHB IB, and AmInvestment Bank. The BursaNews founders worked in investment banking.
A Memorandum of Understanding. A non-binding agreement between two parties that says they intend to work together or do a deal. It is essentially a handshake on paper. An MOU does not guarantee anything will happen. Many companies announce MOUs to positive market reaction, but MOUs frequently expire or fall through. Always wait for a definitive agreement before getting too excited.
When a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time and gets listed on the stock exchange. It is how companies raise money from public investors. Once listed, anyone can buy and sell the shares freely. IPO shares are often oversubscribed, meaning more people apply than there are shares available, so allocation is done by ballot.

Got an IPO Pink Form from your employer? Read IPO Pink Form Malaysia. Applying as the public? See our Bursa IPO guide. You will need a CDS account first.
When a listed company raises money by offering existing shareholders new shares at a discounted price. As a shareholder, you have 3 choices: (1) Subscribe, buy the new shares at the discounted price. (2) Sell your rights entitlement to another investor. (3) Do nothing, let the rights lapse. If you do nothing, your shareholding percentage gets diluted because more shares are now in circulation.

When a listed company issues new shares directly to selected investors, usually institutions or high-net-worth individuals, without offering them to the general public. It is a faster way to raise money than a rights issue. The downside for existing shareholders is dilution. The price is usually at a small discount to the market price.
Free extra shares given to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings. For example, a 1-for-2 bonus issue means for every 2 shares you own, you get 1 free extra share. The total value stays roughly the same because the share price adjusts down proportionally. It is not free money, but it makes the share price lower and more accessible, which can improve liquidity.
The Central Depository System account where your Bursa Malaysia securities are recorded electronically. You open it through a stockbroker or the Bursa Anywhere app. Some brokers give you a CDS account in your own name, others hold shares through a nominee account.

Your account with a licensed stockbroker that lets you place buy and sell orders. It works together with your CDS account: the trading account places the order, the CDS account holds the shares.

The fee your broker charges for executing a trade. On top of brokerage, every Bursa trade also pays a clearing fee to Bursa Malaysia and stamp duty to the government, which are the same whichever broker you use. Brokerage is where brokers differ most.

The application form an IPO company gives its Eligible Persons, usually directors and employees, so they can apply for shares reserved for them. You will usually need a CDS account in your own name, and must follow the instructions in the prospectus.
