3 blind spots retail investors fall into

The problem isn't effort.
It's that you only saw the headline.

The same announcement can mean opportunity to one investor and risk to another, while most only read the headline. The difference is understanding the numbers behind it. All three examples below come from notes we actually sent to subscribers.

Strong earnings, so the share price should rise?

Top Glove's revenue rose 31.9% and net profit jumped 133.1% to RM81mil year on year. Great headline. But glove prices went up because nitrile latex costs rose, not because demand improved, and prices had already started slipping by end May.

The question to askCan this earnings growth last? Is it coming from demand, pricing or costs?

Kelington won a RM128mil contract. Sounds big. But the job runs to April 2028, so the revenue is recognised over roughly two and a half years, and it is under 4% of an orderbook of about RM3.5bil. Contract value is not this quarter's revenue, and it is certainly not net profit.

🔔 Gamuda 最近业绩平平？

截至 2026 年 4 月 30 号，Gamuda 的订单（Orderbook）已来到约 RM50bil，是历史新高，也已经达到管理层设下的目标。

这些订单有转换成盈利吗？答案是没有。 FY2026 1H（上半年）净利 PAT 是约 RM445mil，仅占市场全年预期约 35%-40%。

这并不是订单不够，而是因为：
• 超过 80% 海外项目处于动员期（Mobilization Stage）或施工初期
• 越南 Hanoi 的房地产项目因土地交付延迟，导致销售也被延迟

建筑行业的利润释放通常遵循 S 曲线：前期投入大、利润确认低；进入中后期时，工程进度加速，利润会明显跳升。

◆ 因此，市场预期 Gamuda 盈利爆发期的开始是 end FY2026 - FY2027，也就是大概 2026 年尾开始。

Guidance report: understand what management says about the future

After results, companies usually hold an analyst briefing. What moves the share price is often what management says there, not the results table itself.

What you see in the news

📰Revenue and profit up or down by X%
📰The dividend declared
📰One or two lines of official outlook

What the guidance report tells you

🎯Management's targets for next year's revenue, profit and margin
📦Orderbook, tenderbook, and which big contracts are close to a result
⚠️One-off items behind the numbers and the risks to watch
⏰Upcoming catalysts and the dates to watch
Example (from the Kelington note above)
The news only covered the RM128mil contract. But in early September management hinted they had a higher than usual chance of winning a RM2bil Singapore job and a RM1bil Malaysian job, against a normal win rate of 20 to 30%. That is the kind of insight the guidance report pulls together for you.
Real cases

3 real cases,
3 skills every investor needs

Everyone can read the headline. The difference is being able to see what it leaves out.

Skill 1 · Spotting temporary earnings weakness

Critical Holdings: results collapse. A broken business, or project timing?

What the headline said

Revenue down 47.94% year on year to RM50.8mil, net profit down to RM1.2mil.

What our note pointed out
  • Weak results came from slower progress in the MEP unit, a project timing issue
  • Unbilled orderbook of about RM300mil
  • A RM153mil contract won the week before, with only about 7 months of work, pointing to better results over the next 2 to 3 quarters
  • A net cash company, with no obvious problem in the fundamentals
What you learn

One quarter's results can simply reflect project timing. Read them together with the orderbook, new contracts and the balance sheet to see whether the fundamentals have changed.

Skill 2 · Judging whether earnings can last

Top Glove: profit up 133%. So why did we urge caution?

What the headline said

Revenue up 31.9% year on year to RM1.1bil, net profit up 133.1% to RM81mil.

What our note pointed out
  • Glove prices rose because nitrile latex costs went up, not because of higher demand
  • Nitrile glove prices went from $17 per thousand before March to $28 in April, then slipped to $26 by end May
  • Analysts expected prices to keep easing, and 2 to 3 banks had already cut their target prices
  • The stock's PE averaged about 24x over 3 years, and current earnings looked hard to sustain
What you learn

Good results are not the same as a good trend. Check whether growth comes from demand, pricing or one-offs, and whether it can last.

Skill 3 · Weighing orderbook, margins and valuation

Southern Cable (SCGB): results slip, but the orderbook, cost pass-through and valuation tell another story

What the headline said

Results were heading lower.

What our note pointed out
  • The orderbook recovered: RM1.04bil last September, RM887mil in February, RM925mil in March
  • Contracts allow cost pass-through to customers, but the plastic cost of the cable outer layer cannot be passed on
  • Some analysts expected higher plastic costs to squeeze margins in the second half
  • Forward PE of about 15x, below the 3 year average of 22x
What you learn

Look at the orderbook trend, margin risk and valuation together to judge whether the price already reflects the bad news.
