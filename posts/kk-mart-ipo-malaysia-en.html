Where the money goes: new shares vs offer for sale

At 55 sen the IPO raises up to RM462 million, but only RM115.5 million (from the 210 million new shares) goes to the company. Up to RM346.5 million comes from existing shares sold by K8 Resources and Arus Sejahtera, and goes to them.

KK Mart's RM115.5 million is earmarked for:

  • Distribution centre expansion: RM48.4 million (41.9%)
  • Digital and IT investment: RM28 million (24.2%)
  • New stores: RM25 million (21.7%)
  • The balance for bank loan repayment and listing expenses
💡 About three quarters of the money raised is an offer for sale. That is not automatically a red flag, but most of this IPO's proceeds are not funding expansion.

How the business is doing

  • 996 stores as at 28 February 2026, mostly open 24 hours and concentrated in the Klang Valley.
  • FY2025 (year to 30 June 2025): revenue up 7.7% to RM1.57 billion, but net profit down 4.5% to RM96.98 million.
  • First quarterly results, released with the prospectus: Q1 FY2027 revenue up 7.1% to RM457.95 million.
  • 50.6% of sales happen between 10pm and 10am, so the night trade is a real differentiator.
  • Expansion: 240 new stores in FY2027 and more than 1,500 stores by June 2028.

Valuation: half the price of 99 Speed Mart?

At 55 sen KK Mart is priced at about 19 times trailing earnings, against roughly 38 times for 99 Speed Mart (5326). That discount is the main selling point.

The two are not like-for-like: 99 Speed Mart is far larger and has a listed track record, while KK Mart's profit fell in its last full financial year. Whether the gap closes depends on profit keeping pace with revenue as KK Mart opens about 20 stores a month.

Risks to weigh before applying

  • Tobacco and alcohol: analysis cited by The Star puts tobacco at about 25.5% of sales and alcohol at about 12.9%. Changes in tobacco tax or rules feed straight into revenue. Third-party platforms also show the stock as not Shariah-compliant, which rules out some investors and funds (check the SC's official list).
  • Profit growth: revenue grew in FY2025 but net profit fell.
  • Brand risk: the 2024 socks controversy delayed the IPO and several stores were attacked with petrol bombs. The company says it has since tightened its SOPs and supply chain.
  • Execution: new stores take time to reach mature sales, so fast expansion can weigh on margins in the short term.

How to apply for the KK Mart IPO

  1. Have a CDS account in your own name. Allotted shares go into a CDS account, and only one application per person is accepted per IPO; duplicates are rejected. No account yet? See how to open a CDS account.
  2. Public portion (70 million shares, balloted): apply through a participating bank's internet share application or ATM. The prospectus lists the participating banks.
  3. Through the Moomoo app: Moomoo Malaysia accepts Bursa IPO applications in the app once you have opened a CDS Account-IPO (Malaysian citizens only). If KK Mart appears in the IPO centre, you can apply there. Allow time for account approval, because applications close on 2 December.
  4. Pink Form (35 million shares): only for directors, employees and others who contributed to the group. Read our IPO Pink Form guide first.
  5. Wait for the ballot on 4 December. Money for unsuccessful applications is refunded.

Our take

The best argument for this IPO is the discount to its listed peer. The biggest question is whether profit growth can keep up with store openings. Whether you apply comes down to how comfortable you are with the tobacco and alcohol exposure, the recent dip in profit and the brand risk. We will not tell you to apply, but we would want those three points thought through first.
