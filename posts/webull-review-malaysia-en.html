What Is Webull Malaysia?

Webull was founded in the US in 2016 and has since grown into a global brokerage with tens of millions of users across 16 regulated markets. In Malaysia, it operates as Webull Securities (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd, offering access to Malaysian stocks, US stocks and options, Hong Kong shares, and China A-shares, the latter being a genuinely uncommon offering among Malaysia-regulated brokers, since direct A-share access for foreign investors has historically been restricted.

Is Webull Securities (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd Safe and Legit?

Yes. Webull Securities (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd holds a Capital Markets Services Licence (CMSL) issued by the Securities Commission Malaysia under the Capital Markets and Services Act 2007, and is registered as a Trading Participant of Bursa Malaysia. As with any SC-licensed broker, client assets are covered by the Capital Market Compensation Fund for broker-related default, up to RM100,000 per investor. This is not insurance against your investments losing value; no legitimate broker or regulator anywhere offers that.

New: Webull Flexible RSP (Fixed vs Dynamic Amount)

Webull Malaysia has rolled out its Regular Savings Plan, which the app calls Recurring Investment. You pick a US stock or ETF, choose how often to buy (weekly, every two weeks, or monthly), and Webull places the order for you on schedule using the USD buying power already in your account. It takes the “when should I buy?” decision off your plate, which is where most beginners stall.

How Dynamic Amount works, in plain terms

You set a base amount, then choose a benchmark and a moving-average period. The benchmark can be the stock you are buying itself, or a market index such as the S&P 500, Nasdaq or Dow Jones. Before each purchase, Webull checks the benchmark’s previous close against that average. Trading below the average means a bigger buy; trading above means a smaller one.

So with a USD 50 base amount, a single purchase could be as low as USD 25 when the market is running hot, or as high as USD 100 after a sharp dip. Over time you naturally put more money in when prices are lower and less when they are stretched, without having to make that call yourself.

Notice the asymmetry: the plan cuts back gently when markets run up, but steps in harder when they fall.

💡 Our take as ex-bankers: Dynamic Amount is a disciplined rule, not a crystal ball. A stock trading below its average can keep falling, and buying more of it does not make it a safer company. Pick what you buy carefully first; automation only helps with when and how much.

Costs to keep in mind

Webull Malaysia lists zero commission and no platform fee on US stocks and ETFs, which is what makes small weekly or monthly buys sensible here. On many other brokers, a minimum fee per trade would eat a big slice of a USD 20 purchase. That said, zero commission is not zero cost. You still pay the ringgit to USD conversion spread, any regulatory and exchange fees that apply, and an ETF’s own annual expense ratio. Currency movement also affects what your holdings are worth in ringgit.

How to set one up

  1. Fund your Webull account and convert to USD, so there is buying power ready before the first buy date.
  2. In the Webull app, go to Markets, then Discover, then RSP, and search for the stock or ETF you want.
  3. Set your amount, pick weekly, bi-weekly or monthly, and choose your investment date. Then select Fixed Amount or Dynamic Amount.
  4. For Dynamic, select the benchmark and the moving-average period, then confirm.

If you go Dynamic, keep a cushion of up to twice your base amount in USD. A purchase that doubles on a dip will not go through if the cash is not there. Webull sends a push notification the day before each scheduled buy, and you can update or cancel the plan any time before it is processed.

Which one should you choose?

If you are starting out and want a steady monthly habit, go with Fixed Amount. It is simple and easy to budget. If you understand moving averages and can handle contributions that swing from month to month, Dynamic Amount lets you lean in a little harder during pullbacks. Either way, a broad, low-cost US index ETF is a more forgiving place to start than a single volatile stock.
